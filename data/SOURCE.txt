loan.csv holds 887,379 loans and 74 columns. LCDataDictionary.xlsx defines every
column on its "LoanStats" tab and is tracked alongside the data. Neither file is
committed: loan.csv is 441 MB, well past what a repository should carry, so
`.gitignore` excludes the directory's contents and this record is what the
repository keeps. The three checksums above reproduce the exact files.

Integrity check (asserted in src/data.py on every run): loan.csv's MD5 matches
the value above before it is read; its shape is exactly 887,379 x 74; and every
value of loan_status falls in the set of statuses config.py enumerates, so a
status added by a later LendingClub release fails the run rather than being
silently dropped into the majority class.

Target: loan_status. Only a terminal status carries a known outcome.
  default (positive class, 47,228 loans in the resolved set)
      Charged Off, Default,
      Does not meet the credit policy. Status:Charged Off
  repaid (negative class, 209,711 loans in the resolved set)
      Fully Paid, Does not meet the credit policy. Status:Fully Paid
  unresolved (excluded, 630,440 loans)
      Current, Issued, In Grace Period, Late (16-30 days), Late (31-120 days)
Treating "Current" as a non-default is the standard way this file is misused. It
labels a loan that has not yet had the chance to default, and it is the single
largest status in the file.

Cohort construction, and what each filter costs:
  887,379  all accepted loans
  256,939  resolved outcome                          18.38% default
  178,694  resolved but full term not yet elapsed    20.56% default   EXCLUDED
   78,245  analysis cohort                           13.40% default
The maturity filter keeps a loan only if its full contractual term had run by the
January 2016 snapshot: a 36-month loan issued in December 2012 qualifies, one
issued in January 2013 does not. Without it the cohort is enriched with loans
that resolved unusually fast, which is why the excluded rows default at 20.56%
against the kept rows' 13.40%. That 7.2-point gap is a property of the snapshot
rather than of any borrower.

Analysis cohort: 78,245 loans issued June 2007 through December 2012 (67 distinct
issue months), 10,486 defaults, 13.40% default rate, an imbalance ratio of about
1 default to 6.5 repayments. Default rate by year of issue: 26.2% (2007), 20.7%
(2008), 13.7% (2009), 14.0% (2010), 10.6% (2011), 13.5% (2012). It is not
stationary, which is what makes a forward-chaining validation split disagree with
a random one.

Excluded from the feature set (src/config.py):
  POST_ORIGINATION (16 columns) recorded after the loan was funded. Each is a
    function of what the borrower went on to do. recoveries is the clearest case:
    it is non-zero for 72.8% of defaults in this cohort and for no repaid loan at
    all, and total_rec_prncp is below the amount lent for 100% of defaults.
  NOT_YET_COLLECTED (21 columns) that LendingClub began recording partway through
    the cohort. tot_cur_bal, tot_coll_amt, and total_rev_hi_lim are 100% missing
    before 2012 and 52.0% missing in 2012, so their blankness is a vintage
    marker, and vintage is nearly a default-rate label.
  NON_PREDICTIVE identifiers, free text, and constants: id, member_id, url, desc,
    emp_title, title, zip_code, policy_code, application_type, pymnt_plan,
    acc_now_delinq, initial_list_status.
The post-origination columns are still read, once, for one purpose: pricing what
an error costs (src/data.py, price_errors). Using realized cash flows to value a
mistake is legitimate; using them to predict it is not.

Informative missingness, kept and flagged rather than filled away:
  mths_since_last_delinq   60.8% blank, meaning no delinquency was ever recorded.
                           Those borrowers default at 12.8% against 14.4% for
                           borrowers with a recorded delinquency.
  mths_since_last_record   94.4% blank, meaning no public record. Borrowers with
                           one default at 21.2% against 12.9% without.
Both are filled at a ceiling beyond the observed range and carry an indicator.
Every other gap is under 3.4% and is filled from the training partition's median.

Cost model, estimated from this cohort's realized cash flows:
  loss given default   0.5621 of principal (mean), 0.5845 (median)
  interest earned      0.1671 of principal on a repaid loan (mean)
  mean loss per default        $6,072   on a mean principal of $10,778
  mean interest per repaid     $1,877   on a mean principal of $10,858
A missed default therefore costs 3.24 times what a false alarm costs, which puts
the cost-optimal decision threshold near 0.24 rather than at 0.5.

Partitioning: one 70/30 hold-out, 54,771 training and 23,474 test loans,
stratified on the outcome under master seed 20260815, with both partitions left
in issue-date order so the forward-chaining protocol is a split in time rather
than in row position. Imputation constants, one-hot levels, target encodings, the
scaler, every resampling step, and every decision threshold are estimated inside
the training partition only.
